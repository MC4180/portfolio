Designing an FSAE Aerodynamics Package

I developed the first ever aerodynamics package for Northeastern Electric Racing. I had to reach certain milestones in order for this to be possible: CFD, composites structural analysis and fabrication, and a strong base vehicle to develop around.

The goal of an FSAE aerodynamics package is simple: create downforce to extract more performance from the tires. However, at the relatively slow speeds FSAE cars run, aerodynamic devices are hard-pressed to find meaningful performance gains. Therefore, careful control of the flow structures and maximization of ground effect interactions is required.

I developed the front wing first due to its restricted rules box. I tested various stackups of Benzing, NACA, and Selig airfoils; the Benzing and Selig airfoils tended to be more sensitive in ground effect. I decided on a stackup of three NACA 6412 airfoils for stable, predictable performance across all vehicle states.

I performed 2D geometry optimization in ANSYS Fluent using parametric design tables and custom python scripts. I started by splitting the airfoil stackup geometry into chord length, angle of attack, overlap, and step. These dimensions were parameterized and imported into ANSYS. Because there were issues with solver initialization in ANSYS, I wrote a python script to automatically refresh the geometry, remesh, and recalculate every iteration for greater accuracy and precision. In total, I could quickly and automatically iterate through geometries by specifying a single baseline.

Particular care was taken to produce two counterrotating vortices off the endplates to manage the front tire wake. This prevented dirty air from being upwashed into the rear wing and reducing the overall downforce. In future iterations I plan on further investigating strengthening and outwashing the vortices to increase their usage, as well as creating additional vortices to seal the floor. Additionally, the inherent nature of the Main Roll Hoop creates a large wake that does interfere with the rear wing. Other teams mitigate this by raising the mainplane above the wake, however, I kept the airfoils straight for easier manufacturing.

I designed the wings for best weight:strength ratio using a carbon fiber skin and hollow core with aluminum ribs/endcaps. The carbon fiber skin consisted of a 90/45/90 laminate stackup that provided good torsional and bending strength. An additional carbon fiber spar ran the span of the mainplane airfoils to further assist in bending resistance. I performed FEA on and then lightweighted the airfoils using ANSYS ACP to model the composites and fluid-structure interactions (FSI) to import pressure maps from CFD into the structural analysis.

I designed the front wing mounting using conservative case numbers. It needed to withstand maximum downforce, lateral cornering forces, and drag from the car spinning out. FEA was performed in ANSYS and coupled with topology optimization studies to create a more efficient solution. I added cables to support lateral load, allowing the main mounts to be lighter. The entire mounting solution in total weighs less than 1 lb.

The airfoil molds were manufactured out of MDF as it was a low-volume part. To save time on mold prep, it was sanded and then a layer of mylar applied to provide a good A-side finish and assist with releasing. The skin layups were done with a wet-layup + vacuum bag process, then trimmed and assembled to the internal structure using epoxy adhesive.

The endplates were fabricated using a nomex honeycomb core for good stiffness and weight qualities.

The front and rear wings weighed around 20 lbs in total, exceeding the goal of 25 lbs. However, the manufacturing of the airfoil skins was laborous, and in the interest of quicker manufacturing skinning foam cores could be used in the future.

Track testing showed a 5% gain in laptimes. Despite not being able to compete at FSAE Michigan, this aero package provided valuable developmental data and paved the way for further development (new car coming soon!).
