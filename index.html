Hi, I'm Michael Chang!

I'm a fourth year undergraduate mechanical engineering major at Northeastern University with a specialization in fluid and structural simulations, composite design/fabrication, and rapid prototyping. My interest in engineering has spanned nearly the past decade of my life, encapsulating my first flight lesson at thirteen years of age, to serving as the head structural engineer for Northeastern Electric Racing. What started out as a love for flying turned into a passion for high-performance applications, particularly those involving aerodynamics.

I've had experience designing and manufacturing suspension uprights, bodywork, and an aerodynamics package for an electric race car, metamaterial testing jigs with a commercial stud sensor, a control system with three different modes for a demonstration antenna, and more.

Skills

Mechanical Design and Manufacturing

Throughout high school and college I participated in the mechanical design of dozens of parts and systems: steering columns, airfoils, suspension parts, FRC and FSAE chassis, weld tabs, assembly jigs, etc. I've designed parts for welding, waterjetting, sheet metal bending, CNC machining, laser cutting, and maintenance accessibility, giving me a wide range of experience. I also have good knowledge of materials including metals, composite reinforcements and resins, and SLA 3D printing filaments. As a result, I'm able to rapidly prototype complex parts and quickly learn to improve future designs. My design judgement lies in simple, lightweight systems that can be manufactured efficiently and integrate well with other parts. In addition, I have experience with CAM, CNC mills and routers, manual lathes, waterjet machines, and CO2/IR/diode laser cutters.

Finite Element Analysis (FEA)

I've performed FEA—both in Solidworks and ANSYS—on suspension uprights, swan necks, carbon fiber airfoils, and an FSAE chassis. I've run mesh studies to produce consistent, accurate simulations across both anisotropic composites and isotropic materials, including combined simulations such as airfoil structures. I've always verified simulation results with real world testing on Instron machines and static/dynamic loading tests. In addition, I've built multiple calculators to define force magnitudes and vectors, and taught underclassmen general practices on proper fixturing and boundary conditions.

Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)

Over the past three years, I've taught myself solver and meshing softwares such as ANSYS Fluent, StarCCM+, Fun3D, Pointwise, and GridPro. My experience spans from FSAE race cars to hypersonic vehicles and internal ramjet flowpaths. I've worked extensively to automate meshing and solver workflows for parametric studies, script real-time wind tunnel correlation to simulated models in softwares like Tecplot and ANSYS Ensight, and streamline model generation workflows from CAD to mesh. I've also worked to validate simulations using real-world testing utilizing tools such as strings, pressure taps, strain gauges, etc., and used these results to further refine simulations.

Composite Design and Manufacturing

I'm well versed with designing and manufacturing budget composite parts that still perform with high structural loads and achieve Class-A finishes straight out of the mold. At Northeastern Electric Racing, I developed the team's composites workflow, combining woodworking and bookbinding principles with composites technology, leading a small team of 7 people to develop our wet-lay and infusion layup processes over the course of three years. I used this experience to document mold design guidelines for other projects to use. I've also performed steady-state structural analyses on composite parts using ANSYS ACP to model complex composite systems for FEA with pressure mapping from ANSYS Fluent.
