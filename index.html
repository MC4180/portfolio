Hi, I'm Michael Chang!

I'm a fourth year undergraduate mechanical engineering major at Northeastern University with a specialization in fluid and structural simulations, composite design/fabrication, and rapid prototyping. My interest in engineering has spanned nearly the past decade of my life, encapsulating my first flight lesson at thirteen years of age, to serving as the head structural engineer for Northeastern Electric Racing. What started out as a love for flying turned into a passion for high-performance applications, particularly those involving aerodynamics.

I've had experience designing and manufacturing suspension uprights, bodywork, and an aerodynamics package for an electric race car, metamaterial testing jigs with a commercial stud sensor, a control system with three different modes for a demonstration antenna, and more.

Skills

Composite Design and Manufacturing

I'm well versed with designing and manufacturing budget composite parts that still perform with high structural loads and achieve Class-A finishes straight out of the mold. At Northeastern Electric Racing, I developed the team's composites workflow, combining woodworking and bookbinding principles with composites technology to create strong yet lightweight bodywork and wings. I've also used ANSYS ACP to model complex composite systems for FEA using pressure mapping from ANSYS Fluent. Overall, my contributions have allowed Northeastern Electric Racing to design and analyze lighter, stronger parts.

Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)

Over the past three years, I've taught myself solver and meshing softwares such as ANSYS Fluent, StarCCM+, Fun3D, Pointwise, and GridPro. My experience spans from FSAE race cars to hypersonic vehicles and internal ramjet flowpaths. I've worked extensively to automate meshing and solver workflows for parametric studies, script real-time wind tunnel correlation to simulated models in softwares like Tecplot and ANSYS Ensight, and streamline model generation workflows from CAD to mesh.

Finite Element Analysis (FEA)

I've performed FEA—both in Solidworks and ANSYS—on suspension uprights, swan necks, carbon fiber airfoils, and an FSAE chassis. I'm able to run mesh studies to produce consistent, accurate simulations across both anisotropic composites and isotropic materials, including combined simulations such as airfoil structures. Additionally, I've built multiple calculators to define force magnitudes and vectors, and taught underclassmen general practices on proper fixturing and boundary conditions.
